# Story Arc: The Prisoner in the Dungeon

Design doc, 20 Nov 2026. Not a build step: nothing here is built until it becomes a roadmap step. Read docs/tone.md before writing any line from it.

## The big picture

The party thinks it's fighting an evil plague: poison, rot and nasty tentacles. It's actually the Guardian, the living dungeon trying to keep a wizard locked away, and the party lets him out in Act 2 because he asked nicely.

- **The dungeon is alive.** It speaks in circular runes with no start or end. It doesn't feel time in a line, so it knew the party was coming before they arrived.
- **The wizard is the curse.** The green veins, the fog and the corrupted walls are his magic, slowly poisoning the dungeon. He wanted to see everything, the future included, so he tried to read the dungeon by force. The price was eyes, growing everywhere.
- **The tentacles are the Guardian's jailers.** They were never just monsters. Every time one touched a wall, it was trying to write a word.
- **The party is the villain's key.** In Act 2 they kill the Guardian, free him, and only then learn what they've done. Act 3 is making up for it.

Tone rule holds throughout: the dungeon and the wizard play it completely straight; the comedy comes from the party reacting.

## Act 1, floor 1: learning the ropes

Act 1 is where players learn the game: small monsters, torches, oil, traps and the party's banter.

- Rats, skeletons, slimes, the zombie and its brown-death gas all feel like one evil plague.
- The first tentacle ambushes land as pure scares. Nothing hints they're anything else.
- Rune carvings and wall smears are everywhere, but they read as creepy decoration.
- The Cursed Warden at the stairs closes the act.

## Act 2: the prisoner

In Act 2 the party finds an old man locked in a cell, with the Guardian coiled round it. They kill the Guardian, open the door, and however hard they press him, he eventually walks off.

**The cell.** It looks like a monster lair holding a poor prisoner. Green veins run through the stone everywhere except the cell itself, where they stop dead at the bars. The walls around it are carved with runes, and tentacles are thickest in the corridors nearby. On a first visit, it all reads as "something terrible is keeping this man here."

**The scene**

*A thin figure in a hooded robe, slumped against the bars. Gloves on. Only one eye shows under the hood.*

**PRISONER:** Please. Get me out. It's been so long.

**CLERIC:** A man of faith. I can tell.

**THIEF:** He's got gloves on. In a dungeon.

*The Guardian lies dead. The party finds the key, or pulls the lever. The cell door grinds open.*

*The old man steps out, stretching stiff fingers inside his gloves. The party can press him with questions. Whatever they pick, he answers kindly and tells them nothing.*

**THIEF:** Who locked you up?

**OLD MAN:** The dungeon. It does not like visitors.

**CLERIC:** What were you imprisoned for, my son?

**OLD MAN:** For looking too closely.

**MAGE:** Those runes on the door. Can you read them?

**OLD MAN:** A warning. For the dungeon, not for you.

**FIGHTER:** Where are you going?

**OLD MAN:** Out.

*However long they talk, he looks at the party for a long moment. Then he turns and walks into the dark, laughing softly, and fades away to nothing.*

*The screen shakes. Dust falls from the ceiling. Somewhere deep in the walls, the laugh carries on: louder, deeper, wrong.*

**MAGE:** I don't like this.

**CLERIC:** Relief, surely. The poor man's been locked up for years.

*The laugh stops dead.*

**FIGHTER:** ...That was rude.

**THIEF:** Didn't even say thanks.

**CLERIC:** Charity is its own reward.

**THIEF:** Is it, though?

**The clue nobody can read yet:** the runes around the cell say *do not open*.

## The gift: the green-eyed amulet

The prisoner leaves an amulet in the empty cell; it is genuinely useful, and it is one of his eyes.

**What it does:** a polished green stone on a cord. Worn, it lets the wearer see in the dark, so the party needs fewer torches. In a game where light is a resource, that's a big deal, so players will want to keep it. It follows the legendary rule: the cursed land's best gear comes at a price.

**The slow clues** (spread across Acts 2 and 3):

- Ambushes seem to know where the party is. Monsters find them faster.
- Runes go quiet near the wearer, or appear scratched out.
- The tentacles go for whoever wears it first. They were trying to rip it off.
- Late on, the stone blinks.

**The choice:** keep using it (it's very good) or smash it and go back to fighting for every torch.

**CLERIC** (wearing it, smugly): The Light provides.

**THIEF** (after the blink): The Light just winked at me.

## Through the game: learning the dungeon's tongue

The party learns to read the runes a little at a time, and only understands the Guardian's messages after they've killed it.

**Learning the language.** The same circular runes keep turning up: by curse zones, near ambush walls, outside the cell. Each new rune found becomes a word in a small glossary. The mage translates, nervously and with too many footnotes, and sometimes gets it wrong. Readable runes become warnings ("hungry wall" before the giant hand, "it hates the light here" before a curse zone), so ambush clues turn into actual language. Fluent enough, and the party gets flashes of what's coming, the way Louise does in Arrival.

**FIGHTER:** What's it saying?

**MAGE:** Roughly... "leave". Or "lunch". The tense is ambiguous.

**THIEF:** Ask it where it keeps the gold.

**CLERIC:** I do not negotiate with masonry.

**The tentacles were writing.** On floor 1, the smears they leave on walls look like slime. Once the party can read a little, they look back and see words: *help*, *it hurts*, *the light burns us*.

**FIGHTER:** So I've been stabbing a pen pal.

**MAGE:** In fairness, its handwriting is appalling.

**CLERIC:** I maintain it was evil. Evil can be polite.

**The mistranslation.** The mage reads a rune as *weapon*. The party prepares for a fight. It means *gift*, and a reliquary opens.

**CLERIC:** You might have checked the dictionary.

**MAGE:** There is no dictionary. I am the dictionary.

**The choice.** In one later ambush, the party can lower their weapons and drop the torch. A tentacle grabs a hero and writes a rune instead of squeezing.

**The Guardian.** The Act 2 boss is the Guardian, coiled round the old man's cell. The party kills it to reach him, and it feels like a win.

## The reveal: "Do not open"

With the Guardian dead and the old man gone, the last runes translate, and the party learns what they've done.

*The Guardian lies still. Across the chamber walls, its smears glow faintly green. The mage steps closer, lips moving.*

**MAGE:** It's... the same message. Over and over.

**FIGHTER:** Go on.

**MAGE:** "Thank you. Now he will come."

*Silence. Somewhere far off, the dungeon groans.*

**MAGE:** The runes on the cell door. I've translated them.

**FIGHTER:** Go on.

**MAGE:** ..."Do not open."

**FIGHTER:** Brilliant.

**THIEF:** Gloves. I said gloves.

**CLERIC:** ...He seemed devout.

**FIGHTER:** You sense evil in soup, but not in him?

**What the player now knows:** the tentacles were the dungeon's jailers, and the immune system fighting the curse. The party killed the patient and freed the disease. The warning was there all along; they just couldn't read it yet.

## Act 3: stop him before he gets out

The party opened the door and killed his jailer, so now they have to make up for it: chase the old man through the dungeon and stop him before he gets out. The longer he's free, the more eyes he grows, and the final boss is what's left of him.

**His look.** Still mostly a man, which is creepier than a monster. Eyes in the wrong places: on his palms (the reason for the gloves), his neck, the backs of his hands, along his staff, blinking out of the green veins in his robes. Keep him clearly our own design: not a floating eyeball with eyestalks, and never the word "Beholder" (that monster belongs to Wizards of the Coast).

**Name ideas:** The Seer · Malvorn the Thousand-Eyed · The Watcher in the Mortar · Old Many-Eyes (what the party calls him) · Bewiz (dev nickname only).

**Fight ideas:**

- Eyes open and close; only open eyes can be hurt.
- Some eyes only see in the light, so torch and darkness matter one last time.
- Bless burns eyes shut, so the cleric finally earns his smugness.
- If the party kept the amulet, he knows every move they make.

**First sight**

**FIGHTER:** Which one do I hit?

**THIEF:** All of them. Quickly.

**MAGE:** He's staring.

**CLERIC:** He's staring at you. All of him.

**THIEF:** Oh, it's a wizard. It's always a wizard.

**MAGE:** Not all wizards.

**FIGHTER** (looking at the mage): Every wizard I've met has been trouble.

## Bellius, open questions and the roadmap

**Bellius's part.** The bard who escaped could be the one person who learned to read the dungeon. His torn pages aren't just stories, they teach the party the language, one rune at a time. Maybe he saw how it ends, and that's how he got out. Maybe he tried to warn the dungeon about the wizard.

**How it fits the roadmap**

- Act 1 (floor 1) only needs rune carvings and tentacle smears: a mystery, nothing explained yet. The cell, the old man and the Guardian come with Act 2.
- The tentacles (7d.3) stay a normal ambush on floor 1, so the first scare keeps its bite. Their wall smears can be added as decals now, ready for the reveal.
- The rune language, the glossary and the floor 2 reveal need a second floor, so they wait for Foundations Step 12 and the dungeon editor (Phase E).
